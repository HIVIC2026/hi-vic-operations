Email enquiries plan

  1. Already built: emails from known owners and trades are filed on their job. Emails nobody can match show on Needs me as "Match this email" with the portal's best guess.
  2. Next, new people asking about price or the rebate: the portal replies once, automatically, pointing them to the website calculator, which qualifies them and creates the enquiry itself. No office typing. Never replies to junk or automated mail. TO BUILD
  3. Then, known owners asking where their job is up to: the portal writes the reply as an Outlook draft from the job's live facts. Jo or Chris presses Send. After two weeks of drafts that never need changing, that reply type is switched to send by itself.
  4. Change requests (cancel, can't make a day, new phone number): already shown on Needs me as "Owner asks to … Apply / Ignore". One tap applies it through the normal rules.

Paid live tester plan

  1. Hire one careful person for one day, for example through Airtasker or a local testing freelancer.
  2. They follow the go-live test script on TEST jobs only, playing the owner on a test phone and email, the installer on a TEST installer account with Mitti, and the electrician.
  3. Every message goes to the test inbox and test phone, so no real owner or trade is contacted.
  4. They write down anything confusing or broken with a screenshot. Claude fixes it, and they re-check those steps.
  5. Only after a clean run do the emails and texts get switched on.

The one rule every trade must know

No complete Mitti form, no payment

Installers and electricians are paid for the safety check (A4), the electrical fixes (A5) and the install (A3) only when that Mitti form is 100% finished and they have pressed Complete.

  • Every photo and video is taken in Mitti, on site. That is what geotags and time-stamps it. Photos from the camera roll or taken somewhere else don't count.
  • Nothing can be skipped. The ESC checks this evidence before it accepts our VEEC claim. If one photo, video or answer is missing, we can't claim the rebate.
  • So the pay waits. Until the form is complete and every piece of evidence is there, the payment shows as Held on the Money page with the reason. Once it's fixed, it's paid on the normal terms.
  • Tell every trade this up front when they start, and again whenever a form comes back incomplete.

Two ways a job comes in

From the moment the owner accepts the quote, both kinds of job follow the same steps. Before that they differ.

A1 · The installer's own customer
  • The installer adds the job in the portal: owner's name, address, mobile, email and a tick that the owner said we can contact them.
  • The installer picks the electrician once, at Add a job: their usual electrician or ours.
  • The installer arranges the Ready Check visit with the owner directly.
  • The installer's business owner enters the price. Their staff never see prices.
  • We don't mark up the installer's price. We add our AP fee and the electrician's safety check.
  • The installer is paid when the owner pays the final balance.
A2 · Our lead
  • The owner fills in the calculator on our website and gets an estimate.
  • The owner taps Save my spot. The office picks one installer.
  • The installer offers the owner 3 visit times.
  • The price comes from our price list (R5, R6, R7 per m², removal per m²).
  • The installer and electrician must not discuss price with the owner. We send all prices.
  • The installer is paid 7 days after AP approval.

Home Insulation Victoria (HIV) is the VEU Accredited Person (AP) on every job. There are two kinds of job, and they make money in different ways:

What this means
  • On every job, both A1 and A2, we earn the $349 AP fee and the VEEC spread. At today's $85.80 spot price and a $70 owner discount, that is about $15.80 per VEEC, or $11.45 after the ESC lodgement fee.
  • On an installer's own customer (A1), that is all we earn: about $500 net on a typical 120 m² job. We add nothing to their price, their electrician or their fixes.
  • On our own leads (A2) we also earn on the install, removal, access loading, safety check and $100 on fixes. A typical 120 m² job can make $630 to $1,400 net, depending on the installer.
  • Which installer we send our lead to changes our margin more than anything else. On the same job, Reinsulate leaves us about $764 on the insulation and Rapid leaves us about $0. Rapid's R5 rate is exactly our $30 owner price.
  • The difficult-access loading (25%) is now one of our biggest A2 margins. If we pay the installer's own access fee as well, that comes out of it.
  • The VEEC spread moves with the market. If the spot price fell below $70 + $4.35 ≈ $74.35, every job would lose money on VEECs, because the $70 discount is promised to the owner up front.
  • Card fees are small, but on A1 fixes nothing covers them.
  • Cash risk on A2: we pay the installer 7 days after AP approval, even if the owner has not paid the balance yet.

How many texts and emails one job sends

These counts come from the message list (messages.json) plus the code. They count only messages that are built and switched on. Messages that are not built yet (E1, D78, D82, D89, D93, O19, O22, O26, O29, O33, O42, O45, O51) and messages that are only recorded (visit reminders, quote nudges) are left out. Once those are built, the counts will go up.

How segments were counted. Segments were worked out by running the real approved wording functions with typical inputs:

  • an 87-character secure owner link
  • an 81-character /field/<id> link for trades
  • "Sarah", "12 Smith St, Ballarat", "HV-1234", and a $1,120 rebate

The rule is 160 characters for one segment, or 153 per segment after that. If any character is outside the standard text alphabet, the whole text switches to 70 characters for one segment, or 67 per segment after that.

Who can install?

The installer business applies online and the office approves it. The application covers:

  • business details and ABN
  • public liability insurance (see Q4)
  • the list of its installers
  • its electrical partner
  • the area it works in and when it works
  • its rates (R5 install rate, batts removal rate and loose-fill removal rate are required; R6, R7 and top-up are optional)
  • bank details
  • the signed installer agreement

To get our leads, the business must agree to do free Ready Checks.

Each installer person needs:

  • An EEC Certified Insulation Installer (CII) certificate, checked by the office. The office records the level (Full or Provisional) and the expiry date. Once that's done, the person is cleared for crews automatically. If the certificate expires or is rejected, they're blocked again (decision 153).
