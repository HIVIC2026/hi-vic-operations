Who does what, and which VEU form is signed when

The one rule every trade must know

No complete Mitti form, no payment

Installers and electricians are paid for the safety check (A4), the electrical fixes (A5) and the install (A3) only when that Mitti form is 100% finished and they have pressed Complete.

  • Every photo and video is taken in Mitti, on site. That is what geotags and time-stamps it. Photos from the camera roll or taken somewhere else don't count.
  • Nothing can be skipped. The ESC checks this evidence before it accepts our VEEC claim. If one photo, video or answer is missing, we can't claim the rebate.
  • So the pay waits. Until the form is complete and every piece of evidence is there, the payment shows as Held on the Money page with the reason. Once it's fixed, it's paid on the normal terms.
  • Tell every trade this up front when they start, and again whenever a form comes back incomplete.

Two ways a job comes in

From the moment the owner accepts the quote, both kinds of job follow the same steps. Before that they differ.

A1 · The installer's own customer
  • The installer adds the job in the portal: owner's name, address, mobile, email and a tick that the owner said we can contact them.
  • The installer picks the electrician once, at Add a job: their usual electrician or ours.
  • The installer arranges the Ready Check visit with the owner directly.
  • The installer's business owner enters the price. Their staff never see prices.
  • We don't mark up the installer's price. We add our AP fee and the electrician's safety check.
  • The installer is paid when the owner pays the final balance.
A2 · Our lead
  • The owner fills in the calculator on our website and gets an estimate.
  • The owner taps Save my spot. The office picks one installer.
  • The installer offers the owner 3 visit times.
  • The price comes from our price list (R5, R6, R7 per m², removal per m²).
  • The installer and electrician must not discuss price with the owner. We send all prices.
  • The installer is paid 7 days after AP approval.

Which insulation products can we install?

There are eight products, the eight tap options in the Mitti forms:

  • Bradford Gold: R5, R6, R7
  • Fletchers Pink Batts: R5, R6, R7
  • Earthwool: R5
  • ECOWOOL: R5

Four rules apply to every job:

  • The product must be on the ESC product register.
  • It must still be in date on the install day.
  • The insulation installed must add up to at least R5.0.
  • We use R5.0 batts to AS/NZS 4859.1 on every job.

The register copy we hold (19 Oct 2026) has 33 models. Some are lower R-values, and those only count if the total still reaches R5.0. The Earthwool R5 register entry expires on 23 Feb 2027. All the others run to 2029.

Who can install?

The installer business applies online and the office approves it. The application covers:

  • business details and ABN
  • public liability insurance (see Q4)
  • the list of its installers
  • its electrical partner
  • the area it works in and when it works
  • its rates (R5 install rate, batts removal rate and loose-fill removal rate are required; R6, R7 and top-up are optional)
  • bank details
  • the signed installer agreement

To get our leads, the business must agree to do free Ready Checks.

Each installer person needs:

  • An EEC Certified Insulation Installer (CII) certificate, checked by the office. The office records the level (Full or Provisional) and the expiry date. Once that's done, the person is cleared for crews automatically. If the certificate expires or is rejected, they're blocked again (decision 153).
  • Full CII for the lead installer. The pre-start check refuses a lead with only a Provisional certificate. This is our own rule, stricter than the ESC's.
  • The ESC privacy collection notice, read and ticked by them personally. Each person gets their own secure link and types their name. Nobody can tick for someone else. Reminders go at 3 and 7 days. Until they tick it, they can't be lead or crew on any install (decision 130).
  • A signature on the job's safety plan (SWMS) before work starts. The plan is on the portal job page (decision 261).
  • A White Card. The installer agreement says every installer needs a current one, recorded by us. Not settled yet: there's no White Card question or check in the onboarding form or the portal.

Who does which visit:

  • Any staff member listed under the business can do Ready Checks.
  • Install-day forms go only to CII-cleared installers (decision 161).

PIISA training certificate: Jo said it's not required (decision 145). The portal still tracks it for now (compliance checklist item J3).

Who can do the electrical safety check (PIESA) and the fixes?

The safety check must be done by a licensed electrician working for a Registered Electrical Contractor (REC). We can only use an electrician who is fully onboarded with us (decision 303). Fully onboarded means:

  • the REC business is approved and active
  • it has at least one approved electrician
  • it has an active safety-check (PIESA) rate on file

REC onboarding asks for:

  • confirmation they're a licensed electrician, with licence number and expiry
  • REC number and ABN
  • public liability insurance of at least $5,000,000
  • confirmation they can do all three services: the safety check, the fixes, and being on site on install day
  • rates: the safety-check visit, install-day attendance, and a fixes rate card (IC-4 downlight, switchboard, RCD, TPS cable per metre, exhaust guard), plus how they charge for the Certificate of Electrical Safety
  • bank details
  • the signed REC agreement

Staff who work under another REC join by invitation from that REC.

Who gets the job:

  • Installer's own customer (A1): the installer picks once, at Add a job: their usual electrician (only if that electrician is an approved REC) or ours. Anyone else holds the quote with "Electrician not onboarded", and that electrician gets one onboarding invite.
  • Our lead (A2): the installer's usual electrician, if they're an approved REC. Otherwise an approved REC in the area.

Electrician offers now go out automatically, with no office step (decision 269). The electrician has 24 hours to offer the owner 3 times.

Rates: the electrician only ever sees their own agreed rate. What the owner pays for the safety check is covered in Q10.

Fixes: the electrician gives one total price on the A4 safety-check form and ticks what it covers. On our leads we add $100 including GST, spread across the lines (decision 142). On the installer's own customers we add nothing (decision 313).

Source: decisions 142, 157, 173, 269, 302, 303, 305, 308, 313; rec-onboarding.core.ts, rec-onboarded.core.ts, rec-agreement.text.ts; source of truth "Electrician rate vs customer PIESA price".

What insurance and certificates do installers and electricians need?

Installer business

  • Public liability of at least $5,000,000.
  • At sign-up they upload the Certificate of Currency and enter the insurer, policy number and expiry date. The expiry must be today or later.
  • If the cover lapses later, the business gets no new offers and no install can start until a renewed certificate is uploaded. My business shows a warning from 30 days before expiry.

Electrician (REC)

  • Public liability of at least $5,000,000, with the same upload and expiry checks. Staff under a host REC are covered by that REC.
  • Licence number and expiry. Warnings go out at 60 and 30 days before expiry. Once it expires, they get no new jobs.

Workers compensation

  • Both agreements say the trade must carry it "where required by law". There's no set dollar amount.
  • The portal can store a workers comp document, but it doesn't require one.
  • The REC agreement also mentions other insurance "required by law or reasonably appropriate". It sets no fixed product-liability minimum.

Certificates for each installer person

  • CII certificate, office-verified, with level and expiry. Expired means blocked.
  • ESC privacy notice ticked.
  • White Card (agreement only; not checked in the portal).

Not settled yet:

  • The yearly re-check reminder for public liability was asked for (decision 193), and the repo doesn't confirm it's built.
  • There's no required amount or expiry check for workers comp.

What forms do we give out or sign as the AP, and when?

In job order:

  1. Quote acceptance and Customer Agreement (owner, secure link, after the quote). This is our direct contract with the owner. The owner ticks the quote and Customer Agreement, ticks that they've read their consumer rights and the payment terms, ticks "18+ and owner or authorised", and types their name. The signed acceptance and receipt are attached to the "You're in" email (O22). (Decisions 126, 127, 171.)
  2. 30-day tick (owner, before choosing safety-check times). See Q7. (Decision 307.)
  3. Official PIESA (DEECA form)
    • The portal fills it in from the electrician's Mitti A4 answers.
    • The owner then confirms it on their secure link: one tick and their typed name, no code (decisions 296, 306).
    • If fixes are done on a later visit, the A5 form adds the fixes section and the Certificate of Electrical Safety (CoES) number, and the PIESA is filled in again (decision 293).
    • A copy goes automatically to the owner and the installer at least 24 hours before the install (O28, decisions 111, 131).
  4. ESC Owner's Consent Form (ESC template v1, word for word)
    • The owner signs it when they tap an install day, before paying the deposit.
    • It must be done at least 24 hours before the start and signed again if the day changes.
    • Our AP representative confirms Section 3 from the office screen. This isn't automatic.
    • The owner gets a copy (O63). (Decisions 97, 123, 128, 170.)
  5. ESC privacy collection notice: every installer person, before their first install (Q2). They get a copy email (I62).

  • Card surcharges are banned in Australia from 1 July 2026 (RBA). So we never show or charge a card fee. Where card is allowed, we absorb the fee: about $6 on $349. (Decision 140.)

On our leads, the extra $100 on fixes covers that absorbed card fee and our coordination (decision 142). Jo brought Stripe back once that margin was in place (decision 144).

  • Electrical fixes never restart the clock. They must be done inside the same 30 days.
  • If the window runs out, a new official PIESA starts a new 30 days.
  • The installer is only offered days inside the window. The pre-start check refuses an install after the last day.
  • (Decision 316. An older line in the source-of-truth file still says "from Section C"; decision 316 replaces it.)

What happens if the safety check (PIESA) expires, and who pays?
  • A new official PIESA is needed before the install. The installer's screen says "A new safety check is needed. Home Insulation Victoria arranges the new check." The owner ticks the 30-day rule again for the new check.
  • Whoever's move it was pays for the new check. No exceptions. Storm, illness and so on still count as that person's move.
    • If it's the installer, their share comes out of their payout.
    • If it's the owner, they pay $349 again.
    • (Decisions 23, 158.)
  • How the portal decides whose move it was (it records the answer once, with a one-line reason, in the office job history):
    • The installer didn't offer days within 5 days of clearance → the installer.
    • The owner didn't answer an offer → the owner.
    • The installer didn't answer the owner's suggested days → the installer.
    • All the owner's suggested days were outside the window → the owner.
    • Someone changed a booked day → that person, unless the other side then sat on it for more than 5 days. Then it's undetermined and the office decides.
    • (Decision 167 approved the 5-day response time.)
  • The office sees a "30-day window lapsed" watch row.

Not settled yet:

  • The lawyer still has to word this for the owner terms (unfair contract terms); it's an open item.
  • The portal only records who should pay. Nothing charges the owner or takes it off the installer's pay automatically.
  • If the owner's original check was at an approved REC's rate instead of $349, it isn't settled what the owner pays for the repeat.

Other questions staff will be asked

What does the owner pay in total, and what's the rebate?

The quote shows the full price, takes off the VEU rebate, and shows what the owner pays.

The full price is made up of:

  • the insulation: the installer's own price on their own customers (we don't mark it up), or our price list on our leads (R5/R6/R7 per m², set under Money › Prices)
  • removal, if needed: $15/m² on our leads
  • the electrical safety check (PIESA)
  • our $349 AP fee

Electrical fixes are extra, and only if the safety check finds something. The rebate comes off the price straight away; the owner doesn't claim it.

What if the crew can't get in?
  • Ready Check: the installer taps "Couldn't get in?" and picks a reason (no one home, couldn't reach the manhole, not safe, something else). We rebook with the owner and offer it back to the same installer first.
  • Install day: the owner is texted and emailed straight away (O65) with the installer's three new days. Nobody is charged for the trip. The 30-day clock keeps running, and the owner is told when it ends. The office is only alerted on a second no-access on the same job.

Source: decisions 214, 215, 217; trade-view.core.ts.

How is a job cancelled?
  • Open the job, then More › Cancel, or use Actions on the Jobs or Enquiries lists. Give a reason.
  • This closes the job, stops every reminder, releases any held install day and the installer, and keeps the history.
  • If you tick the boxes, the owner gets email O69 and the installer gets email I70. Email only, and never twice.
  • A cancelled job can be reopened from Closed with a reason.

Not settled yet: refunds and cooling-off (lawyer).

When do installers get paid?
  • Their own customers: when the owner pays the final balance. The agreement says within 7 days of the later of our approval and the owner paying in full.
