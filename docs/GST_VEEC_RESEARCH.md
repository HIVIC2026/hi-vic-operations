# GST on the VEEC "discount" and on VEEC sales: research note

Prepared 25 Oct 2026 for Home Insulation Victoria (VEU Accredited Person, Activity 48 ceiling insulation). This is research, not tax advice.

## Plain-English answer

The ATO's published position for the closest equivalent scheme (STCs under the federal Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme) is that the installer pays GST on the **full price before the certificate discount**. The ATO's reasoning is that the owner pays partly in cash and partly by assigning their right to create certificates, so the "discount" is a second form of payment and not a price cut. No VEEC-specific ATO ruling turned up, but the same legal reasoning (non-monetary consideration) carries straight over to VEECs, so GST on the full price (the portal's current method) is the safer and probably correct position. Separately, when the AP sells the VEECs it creates to a buyer, that sale is very likely a taxable supply, so GST is charged on the certificate sale price as well. VEECs do not appear to be "eligible emissions units" (which are GST-free), and nothing suggests they are input-taxed financial supplies.

### 1. Full price or net price?

**ATO: GST and the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme** (QC 105956 "GST and the electricity industry" print section)
- https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/gst-and-the-electricity-industry/gst-and-the-small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme
- https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-f33fb13e-a501-4c3a-a6b9-5ee1c30b1060 (print version)
- Viewed via search extracts on 25 Oct 2026. I could not see the page's own "last updated" date.

The search extracts for this page say:
- "an installer calculates GST liability based on the full price of the system and/or installation – not the discounted or reduced amount."
- In the worked example (installer "Peter", system priced at **$4,400 including GST**, **$600** up-front discount for assigning the STCs), "Peter's GST liability is calculated **before** the reduction in price arising from the 'up-front discount' for the assignment of the right to create the STCs." His GST is therefore $400 (1/11 of $4,400), not 1/11 of $3,800.
- On the owner's side: "John's assignment to Lukas is not a 'taxable supply', as John is not carrying on a business and is not registered or required to be registered for GST." So the agent or installer **cannot claim a GST credit** on the value of the assignment it receives. In a related example, the installer "is registered for GST but cannot claim the GST credit in relation to the $600."
- Older ATO wording (legacy URL https://www.ato.gov.au/business/gst/in-detail/your-industry/gst-and-the-small-scale-renewable-energy-scheme/?page=2) says a sale for GST purposes "includes an assignment of a right", and that "a discounted installation price may reflect assigning your rights to STCs."

**Industry reading of the same ATO position.** Greenbank Environmental, "GST explained" (https://www.green-bank.com.au/resources/gst-explained, date not visible) says the ATO considers the point-of-sale STC deduction "merely another method of receiving payment – it is not a discount of any kind." This is a secondary source.

**Legal basis (general GST law, my summary, not checked against the live text).** Under s 9-15 and s 9-75 of the *A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999*, consideration includes non-monetary consideration, and the value of a supply counts the GST-inclusive market value of that non-monetary consideration. The ATO's general ruling on non-monetary consideration (GSTR 2001-6) applies this "barter" analysis. The assigned right to create certificates is the non-monetary part of the price. A 2011 commentary found in the search said the same in general terms: GST is payable on "monetary payments, as well as non-monetary consideration (ie a barter transaction)".

**Applying this to VEECs.** I found no ATO public ruling, ATO ID, Law Companion ruling or published private-ruling summary that names VEECs, VEET/VEU or NSW ESCs. The ESC's own assignment forms describe the same structure as the STC scheme: the consumer assigns the right to create certificates to the AP "in return" for "an identifiable benefit (e.g. price reduction on a product, free installation or a cash-back arrangement)". See for example https://esc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/Activity%206%20-%20Residential%20-FRM%20-%20Space%20heating%20assignment%20form%20-%20V.%201.4%20-%2020241212.docx (V1.4, 12 Dec 2024). Because the legal mechanism is the same (owner assigns a right in exchange for a lower cash price), the STC reasoning very likely applies to VEECs. This is an inference by analogy, not a VEEC-specific ATO statement.

**Contrary view.** Some installer blogs say GST applies only to what the customer actually pays. One example is a TrueTally electrician guide: https://melbourne.truetally.com.au/blog/electrician-solar-battery-installation-gst-melbourne. These are not authoritative and conflict with the ATO's worked example.

**One possible nuance.** If the contract is structured so the AP pays the owner a **separate amount** for the assignment (a cash-back) rather than taking it off the price, the outcome is the same in substance. GST is on the full installation price, and the AP gets no credit for the amount paid to a private owner. Some advisers argue that a contract which genuinely sets a lower price and makes the assignment a condition of that price could change the answer. Nothing found supports that argument for STC-type schemes, so do not rely on it without advice.

### 2. Is the AP's sale of VEECs a taxable supply?

- **Creating** the certificates is not a supply. The ATO says (for STCs) that "no GST is payable when you register or create an STC on the REC Registry". The **sale or transfer** of certificates by a business "may have GST consequences", and the older ATO page says the STC supply is "not GST-free or input taxed". A GST-registered AP selling VEECs in the course of its business therefore makes a **taxable supply**, and the buyer (a retailer or trader) claims the credit.
- **Eligible emissions units.** Secondary sources describe "eligible emissions units" (s 195-1) as GST-free under s 38-590 (Subdivision 38-S). Those sources list ACCUs and Safeguard Mechanism Credit units, and international units, under that definition. See https://www.nationalaccounts.com.au/blog/accu-tax-div-420/ (2026) and RSM, https://www.rsm.global/australia/insights/tax-insights/australian-tax-implications-carbon-farming-and-trading. Neither mentions VEECs, STCs or state energy-efficiency certificates. I could not open the s 195-1 text to confirm the definition is limited to Commonwealth units, but nothing found suggests VEECs are included.
- **Financial supplies.** I found nothing suggesting VEECs fall within the financial-supply list in GST Regulations reg 40-5.09. The ATO treats the closest analogue (STCs) as taxable, not input taxed.
- **Market practice.** I could not confirm from the sources whether quoted VEEC spot prices (for example ~$94.50 in April 2024, per an EnergyAustralia submission to the ESC) are ex-GST. Certificate prices are usually quoted ex-GST with GST added on the invoice, but check the buyer agreement.

### 3. Practical effect on the example job

Example: full price $4,298 inc GST, VEEC incentive $1,400, owner pays $2,898, AP sells 20 VEECs at $85.80.

| Basis | GST on the installation |
|---|---|
| Full price (ATO STC position, portal's current method) | $4,298 ÷ 11 = **$390.73** |
| Net price | $2,898 ÷ 11 = **$263.45** |
| **Difference per job** | **$127.27** (= $1,400 ÷ 11) |

On top of that, the VEEC sale: 20 × $85.80 = $1,716.
- If $85.80 is **ex-GST**, the AP invoices $1,716 + $171.60 GST = $1,887.60 and remits $171.60.
- If $85.80 is **GST-inclusive**, GST is $1,716 ÷ 11 = $156.00.

The AP gets **no input credit** for the $1,400 assignment received from a private (unregistered) homeowner.

So on the ATO-consistent view, GST effectively arises twice: once on the $1,400 of value inside the installation price, and again when the certificates are sold. This is the known "double GST" cost of point-of-sale certificate discounts. On this job it is about $127 more than a net-price approach would give. The portal's full-price method matches the ATO's published STC position. Moving to net price would under-report GST by about $127 per job of this size, unless an accountant or ruling supports it.

## Update 25 Oct 2026 (second search)

- The ATO page "GST and the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme" was last updated **11 December 2025** (per search extract). Its second worked example: system $7,700 inc GST, $1,000 off for assigning the STCs, owner pays $6,700 — the installer's GST is **$700 (1/11 of $7,700)**, and it gets **no GST credit** for the $1,000 because the homeowner isn't GST-registered. A credit is only available when the owner is GST-registered (business system) and gives the installer a tax invoice.
- Searches for a VEEC-specific ATO ruling, private ruling, ESC guidance or IPART (NSW ESC) guidance found **nothing**. The ESC fee notice (creation fee A$4.35 from 1 Jan 2026) doesn't say whether fees include GST.
- Conclusion unchanged: charge GST on the full price before the VEEC discount (what the portal does), GST on VEEC sales, no credit for the assignment from a private owner. Accountant to confirm; a private ruling is the only way to be certain.

## What to confirm with your accountant

- Confirm the STC treatment (GST on the full price before the certificate discount, no credit for an assignment received from a private owner) applies equally to VEECs under VEU. If you want certainty, consider a private ruling, because no VEEC-specific ATO guidance was found. Also ask whether business or GST-registered owners change the picture: their assignment may be a taxable supply, so the AP could claim a credit and would need a tax invoice from them.
- Confirm VEEC sales are taxable supplies (not GST-free "eligible emissions units" under s 38-590, and not financial supplies), and whether the buyer's per-certificate price ($85.80) is ex-GST or GST-inclusive.
- Check that quotes and tax invoices show the full GST-inclusive price, the VEEC incentive as a separate line, and GST calculated on the full price, and that BAS reporting matches.
